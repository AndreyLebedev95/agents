# Professional conduct

Read before in-person or cross-cultural engagements.

## Greetings

When meeting someone for the first time, or greeting someone you only see occasionally, a handshake with eye contact signals friend rather than foe and forms a bond between two people.

**Getting it right.** Firm, but not overpowering. Look the person in the eye — looking away while shaking someone's hand reads as disrespect, and most people notice. Two or three seconds is all you need; do not keep it going too long.

**Do not overdo it.** Arriving at the office every morning and shaking everyone's hand is weird enough to make people uncomfortable. A monthly meeting with the head of another department is exactly the right moment to stand up, say *"Hello, Ruth, nice seeing you again"*, and offer a quick firm handshake. Knowing when to shake hands and when not to is part of the art.

**Cultural variation is real.** A handshake is an acceptable greeting in many places and not in others — some cultures use different greetings entirely, such as bowing, where the timing of the bow itself carries meaning. Find out before you travel rather than after.

## Boundaries

A handshake is a professional way of forming a physical bond. Because that is a good thing, some people assume that a hug would be better and more bonding.

It is not. Hugging in a professional setting, regardless of the environment, can make people uncomfortable and could potentially constitute workplace harassment. The same holds for general conduct in the workplace and when travelling with a coworker.

Skip the hugs, adhere to commonsense professional conduct, and stick with handshakes.

## Reading the room

An effective leader pays attention to verbal and nonverbal signs — facial expressions, body language — and recognizes when it is time to back off.

This matters in two directions. It is how you notice masked skepticism in a meeting, where someone privately disagrees but will not say so. And it is how you know when a conversation you have opened, however well intentioned, is not welcome.

Where a team member arrives visibly bothered, an offer of company on a coffee run creates an opening for a personal conversation and possibly for mentoring. It also creates an easy exit if they do not want one. Offer the opening; do not press through the signals.
